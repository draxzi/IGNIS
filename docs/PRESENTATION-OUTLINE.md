# IGNIS presentation outline

1. **Problem** — Combustion in microgravity behaves differently because buoyancy-driven flow, flame shape and transport change; relevant research is distributed across source pages and investigation records.
2. **Scientific motivation** — Reduced gravity can help isolate combustion phenomena while spacecraft fire safety demands careful interpretation of flame, smoke, suppression and material behaviour.
3. **Solution** — IGNIS is a research cockpit that keeps source links, analysis tools, qualitative safety context and limitations in one place.
4. **Technology** — Next.js App Router, React, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, Recharts, Lucide React, a local typed source index, browser-side CSV analysis and an optional server-only AI adapter.
5. **Data sources** — NASA Glenn Research Center, NASA Physical Sciences Informatics and NASA Technical Reports Server. Synthetic data is explicitly marked.
6. **Major features** — Overview dashboard, Research Explorer, Combustion Data Lab, Comparison Studio, Assistant, Fire Safety Intelligence, Visualization Studio and methodology documentation.
7. **Demonstration** — Search `FLEX`, open a source record, load the synthetic lab sample, switch the chart to oxygen, compare ACME/FLEX/Saffire, ask why buoyancy matters, then open the safety limitation note.
8. **Limitations** — The index is not exhaustive, the demo series is not NASA data, and the app does not certify spacecraft safety or predict emergencies.
9. **Future work** — Ingest documented public datasets with provenance, add uncertainty-aware analysis, add independent account/database configuration and expand tested NASA source coverage.
